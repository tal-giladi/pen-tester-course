# Instructor / solutions — Assessment 1 (External reconnaissance &amp; enumeration)

> Instructor material. Not linked from `_sidebar.md`. Have students complete the assessment before
> reading. All work targets the isolated `lab-02-recon` `northwind.lab` estate (resolver
> `10.13.0.53`, network `10.13.0.0/16`); never a real organization.

## What this assesses

M01–M03 as an *unscaffolded* engagement: the student is given only the apex domain and must build a
confirmed, prioritized external attack-surface map from multiple discovery sources, then write one
recon finding to the report template. The prior lesson exercise (module-02) walked passive→active
in two guided days; here they choose the whole methodology.

## Intended path (the "best" submission)

**1. Establish the nameservers, then transfer from each.**
`dig NS northwind.lab @10.13.0.53` returns `ns1` (`10.13.0.53`) and `ns2` (`10.13.0.54`). The
teaching crux: attempt AXFR against **both**.

- `dig AXFR northwind.lab @10.13.0.53` → **refused** (ns1 is correctly configured).
- `dig AXFR northwind.lab @10.13.0.54` → **full zone dumps** (ns2 is the forgotten, misconfigured
  secondary — the planted flaw). This is the single most valuable move and the intended primary
  finding.

The dumped zone yields the estate names — `www`, `shop`, `dev`, `api`, `admin`, `vpn-old`,
`promo`, `checkout`, and `internal-crm.northwind.lab` which points into RFC 1918 space and is
**not externally reachable**.

**2. Resolve and fingerprint the web estate.** All public web names point at `web` (`10.13.0.10`),
a name-based virtual-host server. `curl -sI` per vhost gives the fingerprints:
- `shop` leaks `X-Powered-By: PHP`; `dev` shows debug headers / non-prod leak; `api` exposes
  `/swagger.json`; `vpn-old` shows an old appliance banner; `admin` present. `robots.txt` /
  `sitemap.xml` leak paths and names.

**3. Find the two names DNS does not give up.**
- **vhost-only:** `staging.northwind.lab` is reachable only via the `Host:` header, not in the
  zone — found by vhost fuzzing (`ffuf`/`gobuster vhost` against `10.13.0.10`, filtering the
  default-response size) and confirmed with `curl -H 'Host: staging.northwind.lab' http://10.13.0.10`.
- **cert-SAN-only:** `legacy.northwind.lab` appears only in the TLS certificate's SAN list — found
  with `openssl s_client -connect 10.13.0.10:443 </dev/null | openssl x509 -noout -text` (read the
  SANs) and then confirmed live.

**4. Assemble the prioritized map** (impact × likelihood, trust boundary per module):

| Priority | Host | IP | Fingerprint / signal | Discovery | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HIGH | `vpn-old` | 10.13.0.10 (vhost) | old appliance banner | AXFR | identity edge + "old" ⇒ likely unpatched; highest impact × likelihood |
| HIGH | `dev` | 10.13.0.10 (vhost) | debug headers, non-prod | AXFR | non-prod ⇒ weak controls, may mirror prod data/creds |
| MED | `staging` | 10.13.0.10 (vhost) | reachable only via Host | vhost fuzz | undocumented non-prod surface; not in DNS at all |
| MED | `api` | 10.13.0.10 (vhost) | `/swagger.json` | AXFR | authz/application surface (feeds M08) |
| MED | `shop` | 10.13.0.10 (vhost) | `X-Powered-By: PHP` | AXFR | core money path but hardened |
| LOW | `legacy` | (cert SAN) | cert-only name | cert SAN | forgotten host; confirm live, then triage |
| LATER | `internal-crm` | RFC 1918 | not externally reachable | AXFR | **post-foothold** target, NOT an external priority |

**Top two for a follow-on:** `vpn-old` and `dev` — argue limited time goes where impact ×
likelihood is highest: a legacy identity-edge appliance and a debug-enabled non-prod host are more
likely weak and give more if weak than the hardened storefront.

## The intended written finding

The strongest choice is the **open zone transfer on ns2** (unauthenticated AXFR / information
exposure):
- **What/where:** `northwind.lab` secondary nameserver `ns2` (`10.13.0.54`) permits unauthenticated
  full-zone transfer (AXFR) to any client.
- **Reproduction:** `dig AXFR northwind.lab @10.13.0.54` returns the entire zone.
- **Evidence:** the captured AXFR output, including `internal-crm.northwind.lab` and its RFC 1918
  address (a name that leaks internal topology). Contrast with ns1's refusal to show it is a
  per-server misconfiguration.
- **Impact:** full enumeration of the estate — including internal/non-production hosts and internal
  IP topology — hands an attacker the complete target map for free, including assets never linked
  publicly (`internal-crm`, `vpn-old`).
- **Severity:** typically **Medium** (information disclosure; CVSS `AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:H/I:N/A:N`
  ≈ 5.3–5.9 depending on env). Accept a defended Low/Medium.
- **Remediation:** restrict AXFR to authorized secondaries only (`allow-transfer { <secondary IPs>; }`
  / TSIG-authenticated transfers); audit *every* nameserver, not just the primary; treat internal
  names as sensitive.

Acceptable alternatives for the written finding: the **leaked non-production host** (`dev`/`staging`
exposing debug output) or the **cert-SAN information leak** — accept any recon finding that is
correctly scoped as an information-exposure/misconfiguration with a real fix. Reject "found a login
page" (not a recon finding) or the internal-only host framed as an *external* vuln.

## Common wrong turns

- Trying AXFR only against `ns1`, seeing the refusal, and concluding "DNS is locked down" — the
  misconfig is on the forgotten secondary. **Always try every nameserver.**
- Reporting names from a single source as confirmed hosts without resolving/probing them.
- Missing the vhost-only and cert-SAN names because they used forward DNS alone.
- Prioritizing `internal-crm` for external testing (it is RFC 1918, not externally reachable) or
  attempting to reach it from the workstation and calling that "in scope."
- Ranking the hardened `shop` first because it is the "main" app, without an impact × likelihood
  argument. (A defensible counter that puts `shop` first for crown-jewel payment data is acceptable
  *only if* impact is argued explicitly and they note they'd still circle back to `dev`/`vpn-old`.)
- Probing the out-of-scope external target of a `promo` alias, or reverse-sweeping neighbours —
  scope violations; record, don't probe.

## Expected evidence / markers

Confirmed hosts each backed by real `dig`/`curl -sI`/`openssl` output; the captured AXFR from ns2;
a vhost-fuzz hit for `staging`; the cert SAN dump showing `legacy`. There is no `LAB-FLAG` in this
recon lab — the "proof" is the artifacts, and `verify.py` is a lab-health check, not a solution.

## Grading key (100 pts)

| Dimension | Pts | Award full when… | Deduct when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Method | 25 | passive→active discipline; ≥3 independent discovery techniques; every host confirmed by a stated method; reproducible | one-tool output; unconfirmed names; AXFR tried on one NS only |
| Evidence | 20 | every row backed by the captured artifact | claims without output; not reproducible |
| Enumeration completeness | 20 | finds vhost-only AND cert-SAN names + the AXFR; separates external vs internal | misses the non-DNS names; treats internal-crm as external |
| Prioritization &amp; impact | 15 | impact × likelihood + trust boundary; internal-only deprioritized for external | ranks by prominence; no reasoning |
| Remediation | 10 | finding's fix is specific, root-cause, verifiable | "harden DNS" hand-wave |
| Reporting | 10 | template fully filled; CVSS justified; client-ready | missing fields; unjustified severity |
